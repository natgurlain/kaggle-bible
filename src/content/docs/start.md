Kaggle Bible is organized around decisions. Read just enough evidence to choose a validation design, a first baseline, and the next experiment you can afford.

## Your first loop

1. Pick a competition from the [beginner-to-advanced path](/path/) or [competition catalog](/competitions/).
2. Read the problem, data, metric, and validation sections before looking at model recipes.
3. Establish a cheap baseline and write down the split, seed, runtime, and score.
4. Use the guide’s evidence cards to choose one change at a time.
5. Record what happened, including negative results, before trying the next idea.

## Read evidence labels carefully

- **Source-reported** means the claim appears in a linked write-up, notebook, discussion, or official competition material.
- **Reproduced** means the result was rerun by a contributor with enough detail to describe the environment and evaluation.
- **Editorial interpretation** means a documented synthesis by Kaggle Bible; it should point to the underlying evidence and state its limits.

If a guide does not say how a result was obtained, treat it as a lead rather than a dependable benchmark.

## Choose the right depth

Level 1 is enough to discover competitions and decide whether one fits your goal. Level 2 helps you form hypotheses from several sources. Level 3 is the reference page to revisit while implementing a solution.

Start with the [catalog](/competitions/) when you need a broad search. Start with [best practices](/practices/) when you already have a competition and need to diagnose an experiment.
